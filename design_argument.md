# Design Argument

## Who is this person?

Ashlee Wang is a college student and a close friend of mine. She manages coursework across multiple classes each quarter, producing dozens of documents, drafts, and research files weekly.

## What's broken for her?

Ashlee saves everything to her desktop or Google Drive with no consistent organization system. When she needs to find a previous assignment, she resorts to searching by keyword or filename — if she even remembers either. Her desktop is cluttered, her Drive is a flat dump of files, and she loses time every week hunting for things she already made.

From our interview:

> "每次保存东西就直接放桌面，后来要找的时候很麻烦。"
> ("Every time I save something I just put it on the desktop. It's hard to find later.")

Her workflow: open the assignment page → search for reference material → open a blank doc → brainstorm → write a final draft. But there is no step for organizing what she produces. Files accumulate. Context is lost. The next time she needs something, she opens the system search bar and guesses at keywords.

## What does "helped" look like?

Ashlee can see all her Google Drive files grouped by course without manually creating and maintaining folder structures. She opens WorkPuzzle, sees her courses, clicks into one, and the files relevant to that class are right there. No searching. No guessing. No reorganizing.

## Why am I the one building this?

Because I know Ashlee. I've watched her scroll through her desktop looking for files. I've heard her complain about it. I'm not designing for a persona — I'm designing for someone I can sit across from and test with directly. And as a student myself, I understand the problem from the inside.

## What this project is not

This is not a general-purpose file manager. It is not a replacement for Google Drive. It is a single-purpose view layer that organizes existing Drive files by course schedule — built for one person, tested by one person.
